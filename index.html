Overview · the architecture

Two chips, one computer

A

BCM2712 does the thinking

CPU, GPU, memory, both HDMI outputs, the SD card, Wi-Fi and the outside PCIe lane connect to it directly.

B

RP1 handles the ports

USB, Ethernet, cameras, displays and the GPIO header all go through Raspberry Pi's own I/O chip.

C

PCIe joins them

A four-lane PCIe 2.0 link carries all of RP1's traffic into the SoC and its memory.

PCIe ×4~16 Gb/s each way between RP1 and the SoC: enough for all ports at once

Chip 3 · southbridge

RP1: Raspberry Pi's own chip

RP1-C0 on your board

On earlier Pis, the main chip ran the ports. On the Pi 5 that job moved to a separate chip Raspberry Pi designed in-house, so the SoC is left to compute.

USB

2 × USB 3.0 + 2 × USB 2.0, from two host controllers

Ethernet

Gigabit MAC, linked to the PHY chip by RGMII

MIPI ×2

Two 4-lane links, each usable for a camera or a display

GPIO

The 40-pin header: UART, SPI, I²C, PWM, I²S and PIO

PCIe 2.0 ×4RP1 appears to Linux as a PCIe device, the same way a plug-in card would.
Chip 4 · power management

DA9091: eight power supplies in one

PMIC + inductors

Read your chips

What the markings say about your Pi

BROADCOM®
2712ZPKFSB00D0T

The D0 means the newer revision of the chip. Broadcom cut silicon the Pi never used, so the die is about a third smaller and idles cooler. Early 4 GB and 8 GB boards used the older C1.

RP1-C0  25/37
PCNA14.00

RP1 revision C0, the version Pi 5 boards ship with. The “25/37” looks like a date code: probably week 37 of 2025, so your board is a recent build.

Raspberry Pi 5
HAT+ GPIO INTERFACE

“Made in the UK” is printed beside the Wi-Fi can: Pi 5 boards are built at the Sony UK Technology Centre in Wales. “HAT+” is the newer add-on standard the header follows.

02

The pathways

Six journeys through the board: a keystroke, a network packet, a file, a video frame, a photo and the power itself.

Pathway 1 · USB input to screen

A keystroke's journey

1

USB 2.0 port

Your keyboard sends a tiny report each time a key changes.

2

RP1 USB host

Its xHCI controller copies the report straight into RAM over PCIe, without the CPU's help (DMA).

3

CPU core wakes up

An interrupt alerts Linux, which passes the key to your app.

4

GPU → HDMI 0

VideoCore VII draws the new frame and the display engine streams it out.

Pathway 2 · networking

Wired and wireless take different roads

Ethernet · through RP1
1

Jack + magnetics

Transformers in the RJ45 isolate your Pi from the cable.

2

BCM54213 PHY

Converts analog cable signals into digital frames (RGMII).

3

RP1 Ethernet MAC → RAM

Frames cross PCIe into memory for Linux to process.

Wi-Fi · straight to the SoC
1

CYW43455 radio

Dual-band Wi-Fi 5 over SDIO; Bluetooth 5.0 over a UART. RP1 is not involved.

Pathway 3 · storage

Three ways to a file

SD

microSD → SoC

Direct SDIO link at SDR104 speed. The default boot drive.

M.2

NVMe → PCIe ×1

Ribbon cable to an M.2 HAT+, straight into the SoC.

USB

USB 3 SSD → RP1

Blue port, then RP1, then the ×4 link.

Pathway 4 · video out

Pixels from memory to your screens

1

Frames live in RAM

Apps and the GPU draw into buffers in main memory.

2

Display engine (HVS)

Combines the layers on the fly and feeds two HDMI 2.0 transmitters inside the SoC.

3

Or a DSI panel

A Touch Display on CAM/DISP is driven through RP1's MIPI link.

2 × 4Kp60both micro-HDMI ports at once, with HDR
Pathway 5 · camera in

How a photo gets made

1

Sensor → CSI-2

Raw pixels stream over up to 4 MIPI lanes on the ribbon.

2

RP1: ISP front end

Receives the stream and gathers statistics for exposure and white balance.

3

PCIe → RAM

Raw frames land in memory.

4

SoC: ISP back end

Demosaics, denoises and colour-corrects. Video is encoded by the CPU cores.

Pathway 6 · power

Where the 27 watts go

1

Negotiate

The Pi asks the supply over USB Power Delivery whether it can give 5 A.

2

Split the 5 V

USB ports, GPIO 5 V pins and the fan take 5 V directly. The PMIC converts the rest.

1.6 Ashared by the USB ports on a 5 A supply; only 0.6 A on a 3 A one
Pathway 7 · from button to Linux

What happens when it boots

1

Power good

PMIC

Brings the rails up in order, then releases the SoC from reset.

2

Boot ROM

inside BCM2712

Code fixed in silicon at the factory. It finds the bootloader.

3

Bootloader

SPI flash on board

Starts the RAM and reads its settings, including the boot order.

4

Find a drive

default: SD → NVMe → USB

Tries each device in BOOT_ORDER until one has a boot partition.

5

Load the kernel

config.txt · device tree

Reads settings, the hardware description and the Linux kernel into RAM.

6

Linux runs

4 cores online

Finds RP1 on PCIe and brings up USB, network and GPIO.

Because the bootloader lives in flash on the board, you can update it with rpi-eeprom-update. Unlike older Pis, the SD card holds no GPU firmware files: it all lives in the 2 MB flash.

03

The edges

The header, the small connectors, the cooling and the underside.

Expansion · J8

The 40-pin header, pin by pin

Oriented as in your photo: pin 1 is on the left, the outer row holds the even pins. All 28 GPIOs use 3.3 V logic: 5 V on a GPIO pin can damage RP1.

Easy to miss

Eight small connectors

Fan 4-pin

4-pin: 5 V, ground, PWM speed and tachometer.

Debug UART 3-pin

3-pin serial console for boot messages.

RTC battery J5

Keeps the clock running while unplugged.

PoE J14

Passes Ethernet power to a PoE+ HAT.

Power button

A first for a Raspberry Pi: clean on and off.

LEDs STAT · PWR

Green for activity, red for power. Blinks spell out boot errors.

Composite J7 · VID

Analog TV output on two solder pads.

PCIe FPC

16-pin ribbon socket for NVMe and AI HATs.

Thermals

Keeping 2.4 GHz cool

Without cooling, a busy Pi 5 gets hot and slows itself down to protect the chip. The official Active Cooler plugs into the fan connector and follows the curve on the left.

Now go use it

Six good first moves

bootloader

Update the flash

Get the latest boot fixes and NVMe support.

sudo rpi-eeprom-update -a
power

Use a 5 A supply

The 27 W USB-C PD supply unlocks the full 1.6 A for USB.

27 W · 5.1 V · 5 A
thermals

Fit a cooler

Plug an Active Cooler into the fan header before heavy work.

vcgencmd measure_temp
storage

Boot from NVMe

Add an M.2 HAT+ on the PCIe ribbon and change the boot order.

sudo raspi-config
clock

Keep the time

A rechargeable lithium-manganese cell on J5 keeps the clock offline. Charging is off by default.

dtparam=rtc_bbat_vchg=3000000
GPIO

Read the pins

The Pi 5's tool to see and set any GPIO's state.
